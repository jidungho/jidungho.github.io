A book-length essay series, written in June 2024 as the talk of the town was shifting from $10 billion compute clusters to $100 billion to trillion-dollar ones, arguing that a few hundred people in San Francisco can see what is coming and almost nobody else can. The core extrapolation is arithmetic: GPT-2 to GPT-4 covered roughly preschooler to smart high-schooler in four years, driven by about half an order of magnitude per year of compute, another ~0.5 OOMs/year of algorithmic efficiency, and "unhobbling" gains that turn chatbots into agents — so one more jump of that size puts AGI around 2027. From there Aschenbrenner argues it compounds: hundreds of millions of AGIs automating AI research could squeeze a decade of algorithmic progress into under a year. The remaining essays work through what that would demand — trillions poured into GPUs, datacenters and a tens-of-percent expansion of US electricity production; lab security he says is effectively handing AGI secrets to the CCP; superalignment as an unsolved problem that a fast takeoff leaves no time for; and, by 2027/28, a government AGI project, on the premise that no startup can hold superintelligence. It is a forecast drawn from trendlines, and he presents it as such.
